NASA Mars 2020 Mission: Perseverance Rover & MOXIE Oxygen Synthesis
Mission Reference: NASA-JPL-MARS2020-SYS-09
Target Site: Jezero Crater, Mars

1. Mission Objectives & Instrumentation
NASA's Perseverance rover successfully touched down in Jezero Crater on Mars on February 18, 2021. Jezero Crater was selected because ancient orbital spectroscopy revealed clay-rich delta deposits indicating a persistent standing body of liquid water billions of years ago. The rover's scientific payload comprises seven sophisticated instruments: Mastcam-Z, SuperCam, PIXL, SHERLOC, MEDA, RIMFAX, and MOXIE.

2. In-Situ Oxygen Production via MOXIE
The Mars Oxygen ISRU Experiment (MOXIE) demonstrated the critical milestone of generating breathable oxygen directly from Mars' thin, carbon-dioxide-dominated atmosphere (95% CO2). MOXIE draws in Martian atmospheric gas, pressurizes it using a scroll pump, heats it to 800 degrees Celsius, and applies solid oxide electrolysis across scandia-stabilized zirconia (YSZ) catalytic membranes.
Across its complete operational campaign of 16 experimental runs, MOXIE successfully synthesized 122 grams of 98% pure breathable oxygen. At peak performance, MOXIE attained an extraction rate of 12 grams of oxygen per hour, doubling NASA's baseline design goals.

3. The Ingenuity Helicopter
Perseverance was accompanied to Mars by the Ingenuity Mars Helicopter, the first aircraft in history to achieve powered, controlled flight on another celestial planet. Originally budgeted for a 30-day technology demonstration of 5 flights, Ingenuity surpassed all projections, logging 72 powered atmospheric flights over nearly three years before suffering terminal rotor blade damage in January 2024.
