📌 Why Recall & PR-AUC Matter in Stroke Triage

In medical informatics, stroke prediction represents a classic high-stakes, imbalanced classification problem. Stroke prevalence in general outpatient cohorts is approximately 4.9%.

  • The Accuracy Paradox: A trivial model that blindly predicts "No Stroke" for every patient achieves ~95.1% accuracy while failing 100% of patients who actually suffer a stroke.
  • High Recall (Sensitivity): In clinical screening, missing a stroke (a False Negative) can be fatal. A screening model must prioritize capturing genuine positive cases.
  • PR-AUC Metric: Unlike the ROC curve which can present an overly optimistic assessment due to a massive True Negative pool, Precision-Recall AUC directly inspects the positive predictive trade-off.

🛡️ Zero Data Leakage Guarantee

Scikit-learn Pipeline & ColumnTransformer

A frequent pitfall in clinical predictive modeling is Data Leakage—when statistical parameters (such as mean imputation values or standard deviation scaling factors) are computed over the full dataset before partitioning into train and test sets.

In our implementation, all imputation, standardization, and one-hot encoding estimators are wrapped within a ColumnTransformer and integrated into an end-to-end Pipeline. Fitting (fit) occurs solely on the 80% training partition. The held-out 20% test partition is only transformed (transform), guaranteeing that test evaluations represent genuine unseen clinical performance.

Clinical Informatics Guidance: Why Emphasize Recall and PR-AUC?

In acute medical screening where the event rate is ~4.9%, conventional Accuracy is inflated by predicting the majority class. Recall (Sensitivity = 80.0%) indicates that out of 50 actual stroke cases in the unseen test cohort, 40 were correctly flagged, leaving only 10 False Negatives. Furthermore, PR-AUC (0.2478) demonstrates a more than 5-fold lift over the random prevalence baseline (0.0489), proving substantial discriminatory capacity under extreme class imbalance.
